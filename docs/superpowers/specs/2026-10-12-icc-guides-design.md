# ICC software guides design

Date: October 12, 2026. Status: proposed design for Owner review; no implementation or scheduling authorized by this document.

## Purpose and agreed requirements

Build a guide library for Canadian quality and operations teams. Each guide explains a software topic through workflows, practical examples, evaluation criteria and implementation. ISO standards support those discussions. The production target is two complete guides per week, in addition to the existing daily blog.

Research must use primary sources and verified ICC product capabilities. Write chapters separately. Use third person or direct address; use ICC's existing US spelling convention. Do not invent features, statistics, testimonials, customer results or quotations. Hypothetical scenarios begin with `**Illustrative example:**` and describe unnamed organizations. Every page needs a unique title, a 120–160-character meta description, a target keyword and a unique image brief. Supply an introduction, FAQs, a software-demonstration invitation, a contents page and previous/next chapter navigation. Review accuracy, repetition, links and consistency before publication.

Pinnacle is a read-only reference. No source code, dependencies, infrastructure, prompts or content are copied from it into ICC. The inspected `/Users/ganesh/Downloads/pinnacle-aws-main` contains QMS document generation, not the public guide pipeline. Its observed writing pattern uses an outline, organizational context, source findings and a writing-style profile. Exact Pinnacle public-guide parity requires a website repository or published guide example. That comparison can refine presentation without changing this independent implementation.

## Current ICC constraints

ICC is a Next.js 14 App Router static export with React, TypeScript, Tailwind and filesystem Markdown. The active blog pipeline selects eligible keywords from `team/memory/keyword-queue.json`, produces a brief and publishes a fixture through a blocking local content gate.

The legacy mega-article path remains switched off. It combines chapters into one Sanity article and includes a publish-despite-failed-QA branch. It is unsuitable for this subsystem. Existing blog product code, routes, publication cadence and frontmatter contract must continue working.

The product source referenced by `lib/site.ts`, `docs/website-content-refresh-prompt.md`, is absent. Establish a maintained ICC capability register before product-specific guide writing. Current website copy is candidate evidence, not independent verification. A feature present in Pinnacle is not evidence that ICC offers it.

The existing local blog gate permits a skipped claims audit when model credentials are unavailable. Guides must not inherit that behavior: a required audit must be completed, current and passing. Its inability to run blocks publication.

## Length and structure

The requested structure permits 9–10 chapters of 2,000–2,200 words, with an 18,000–20,000-word overall target. The introduction also counts toward the overall total. ICC's primary content-page minimum is 1,500 words.

The compatible initial policy is nine chapters, each 2,000–2,200 words, and an introduction of at least 1,500 words. Target 2,000 words per chapter and 1,500 words for the introduction: 19,500 words overall. The sum must remain at or below 20,000; individual chapter maxima do not grant permission to exceed the total. Ten chapters plus the required introduction cannot satisfy the inclusive total, so the initial validator accepts nine chapters and explains this constraint when rejecting ten. Changing that policy requires an explicit change to the requirements.

Count reader-visible Markdown body text, including headings, table cells, FAQ answers and the closing invitation. Exclude YAML, URLs, image briefs, image alt text and navigation generated by the page shell. Compute each page's reading time from its actual count at 200 words per minute. Do not inflate counts with repeated prose or truncate text to meet a limit.

Each chapter has 3–5 top-level body subsections. The page renders its title as its only H1, so these subsections are Markdown H2s. FAQ questions can be H3s within a subsection. Cover 8–10 distinct FAQs across the guide; do not repeat every FAQ on every page. End the last chapter with a natural demonstration invitation linking to the existing `/contact/` page.

## Topic selection

Use a dedicated guide queue. A queued topic includes an ID, software subject, keywords, relevant ICC module slugs, audience, workflow problem, priority and approval status. Seed candidates from ICC module descriptions and workflow problems, then research whether they support nine distinct chapters. Owner-specified topics take precedence and cannot be silently substituted.

Models may suggest candidates but may select only an approved queue entry. Enforce normalized topic IDs, aliases, reserved topics and published-topic checks. A published blog about a subject does not automatically exclude a guide; a guide must provide additional depth and link to useful related articles. Model-generated search volume is never accepted as evidence.

Published manifests are authoritative for completed topics. A persistent draft job reserves an approved topic while it is being prepared. A crash leaves an explicit failed or interrupted job for recovery rather than making the topic disappear from consideration.

## Research and writing

Maintain `team/data/software-capabilities.json`. Each accepted capability has an ID, module, precise allowed claim, limitations, evidence reference, verifier and verification date. Pending and retired capabilities cannot support publication. Store guide research snapshots with primary-source titles, URLs, publisher, retrieval date, supported claims and capability IDs. External URLs must also appear in `team/data/external-link-bank.json`; research enrichment occurs before the scheduled writing run.

A draft contains the research snapshot, complete outline, per-page briefs, introduction Markdown, separate chapter Markdown, job state and review reports. Freeze titles, metadata, slug assignments and the chapter word budget before writing. Writers receive the shared terminology, chapter scope, approved sources, approved capabilities and summaries of completed chapters. Initially write sequentially for continuity; independent chapter parallelization is a later optimization.

Use content hashes to determine which checkpoints remain valid. An edited chapter loses its own review, whole-guide review and relevant navigation/metadata verification. A changed capability or source snapshot invalidates dependent chapters. Image-only edits preserve text reviews only if text hashes are unchanged. Limit automatic correction to two passes per failed review stage, then report the unresolved issues and preserve the draft.

## Publication contract and website

Store a published guide under `content/guides/<guide-slug>/`, with `guide.json`, `introduction.md` and `chapters/<chapter-slug>.md`. Store its images under `public/images/guides/<guide-slug>/`. The manifest controls chapter order and source/capability provenance. Per-page frontmatter supplies title, slug, description, primary keyword, secondary keywords, author, dates and image metadata. Final word counts and reading times are generated, not trusted from a writer.

Routes are `/guides/`, `/guides/<guide-slug>/` and `/guides/<guide-slug>/<chapter-slug>/`, with trailing slashes. The guide root contains the introduction and ordered contents. Every chapter shows the contents link, current chapter and previous/next destinations. The first chapter links back to the introduction; the final chapter has no next link. Reordering chapters does not change their URLs.

Use existing ICC typography and Markdown rendering. Provide a mobile-accessible contents control, visible focus indicators and sensible long-table overflow. Generate static params for all published guides and chapters. Add metadata, canonical URLs, explicit Open Graph and Twitter images, BreadcrumbList and suitable Article/CollectionPage structured data. FAQ markup must match visible questions and answers and is not a promise of search-result enhancements.

Every introduction and chapter needs its own unique licensed 1200×675 hero image. Repeated display of that same page's hero in its own share metadata is permitted; use across different content pages is not. Check provider IDs and image hashes across blogs, other guides and the current guide. Prefer distinct industrial workflow scenes; do not fabricate product screenshots. Image briefs alone are adequate for a draft, but actual processed images are required for publication.

## Release and schedule

Use a separate guide CLI and routine. `publish` means validated local content publication; production deployment requires passing the build and release checks and explicit push authority. It must never overwrite an existing guide or change indexed URLs. Failed QA leaves the existing published site untouched. A build failure after local content publication means stop with uncommitted files, not claim production success or delete an existing guide.

Target two production slots each week; Monday and Thursday are proposed defaults. Exact execution times remain configurable. Business-week accounting uses `America/Toronto` and ISO weeks; show human-facing execution times in the Owner's local timezone, Asia/Kolkata. Timezone conversion must account for daylight saving changes. A late or failed slot is reported; it does not trigger an unbounded backlog run or a weaker gate.

Initially use one designated persistent production host and isolated per-run checkout. Preserve chapter checkpoints on that host. Do not enable an ephemeral cloud routine while claiming cross-run resume unless durable checkpoint storage has been implemented and tested. The two-per-week target concerns complete guide generation; automatic remote publication remains an explicit activation decision. The existing blog standing approval does not cover guide pushes.

No automation is created during planning. Scheduled writers cannot edit code, prompts, queues, capability facts, standards facts, the link bank or QA rules. They may write the assigned Markdown and use authorized guide commands. All remote pushes require a preceding pull; changes introduced by that pull require rerunning the build and relevant checks. Never reset a shared checkout, force push or activate the unrelated legacy agents.

## Acceptance and release boundaries

Acceptance requires a verified research/capability pack; a complete guide passing chapter and overall gates; unique processed images; valid source, chapter, navigation and anchor links; complete page metadata; successful static export; all existing team tests; responsive/device verification; visual regression review; performance, accessibility and security review appropriate to the new pages; and evidence that scheduled retries cannot duplicate topics or publish beyond two weekly slots.

Implementation must not change `app/layout.tsx`, `next.config.mjs` or `team/agents/shared/config.js` as part of the initial design. Put guide settings in a new dedicated module. Before changing any other shared file, inspect graph impact if available, otherwise trace its imports. A file with more than ten importers falls under the Owner's core-file approval rule.

Record guide responsibilities in `AGENT-COMPANY-ORG.md`; that file is currently absent, so establish only verified active roles and the guide additions rather than inventing a full organization history. Add guide role definitions to `teamAuditor.js` without scheduling its legacy dashboard or unrelated agents. Maintain repository documentation describing the new content contract and separate routine.

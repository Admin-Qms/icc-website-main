> **Key Takeaways**
> - ISO 14001:2026 manages the effects a plant has on the environment; ISO 45001:2018 manages the risks a plant creates for the people who work in it.
> - Both standards share the same high-level structure, so one set of core processes can serve an integrated management system.
> - The differences sit in hazards, legal obligations, worker participation and the controls each standard expects.
> - An integrated audit plan can cover both standards in one visit, but each standard still needs its own evidence.
> - Plants can implement the standards together or one after the other; the right order depends on risk, customer pressure and the resources available.

**Illustrative example:** A plant manager who already runs separate binders for spill response, lockout, waste manifests and incident reports may ask whether one system could hold all of it. That question is the starting point for an integrated management system built around ISO 14001 and ISO 45001, and the answer is yes, with some care about where the two standards part ways.

The editions matter here: ISO 14001:2026 is the current environmental standard, published in April 2026, although many certified organizations still hold certificates to the 2015 edition during the transition. ISO 45001:2018 remains the current occupational health and safety standard, with no newer edition published.

## What ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 Each Require

[ISO 14001 Environmental Management](/services/iso-14001) sets out how an organization controls the environmental aspects of its activities: emissions, discharges, waste, energy and raw material use. The organization identifies which aspects can have a significant effect, sets objectives, operates controls and checks the results. [ISO's own explanation of ISO 14001](https://www.iso.org/home/insights-news/resources/iso-14001-explained.html) describes the intent of an environmental management system in plain terms.

[ISO 45001 OH&S Management](/services/iso-45001) does the same job for worker health and safety. The organization identifies hazards, assesses risks, sets objectives, consults workers and operates controls so that injuries and ill health are prevented. ISO's page on [what ISO 45001 is and what it requires](https://www.iso.org/home/insights-news/resources/iso-45001-explained-what-it-is.html) gives the standard's own summary.

Both are management system standards. Neither tells a plant which exhaust filter or machine guard to buy. They require the organization to decide what its risks are, control them, measure the result and improve.

### The side-by-side view

| Topic | ISO 14001 | ISO 45001 |
|---|---|---|
| Core concern | Environmental aspects and impacts | Hazards and risks to workers |
| Who is protected | The environment and the community | Workers and others on site |
| Key legal link | Environmental permits and reporting | Occupational health and safety law |
| Worker role | Competence and awareness | Consultation and participation |
| Typical evidence | Aspect register, waste records, monitoring data | Hazard register, incident records, consultation records |
| Emergency planning | Spills, releases, fires | Injuries, evacuations, exposures |

The table shows why the standards pair well. The structure is parallel, the evidence is different, and the audit questions land in different parts of the plant.

## Where Environmental and Worker Safety Management Overlap

**Illustrative example:** A plant that has already passed one ISO audit may recognize the second standard quickly: context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation and improvement appear under the same headings in both. In practice that means the person who owns the internal audit schedule for one standard can usually run it for the other.

Several processes can be shared without losing anything:

- **Document and record control** can run through one procedure, because both standards expect documented information to be approved, current and available where it is used.
- **Management review** can be one meeting with one agenda, as long as the inputs for environmental and safety performance are both covered.
- **Internal audit** can use one program and one set of trained auditors, provided the audit plan names the criteria for each standard.
- **Corrective action** can use one register, because an oil leak and a near miss both need a cause, an action and a check that the action worked.
- **Competence and training** can share one matrix, with columns for environmental and safety requirements.

> **Key Consideration:** Share the process, not the evidence. One corrective action procedure is sensible, but each corrective action should still be tagged to the standard it affects so an auditor can sample it quickly.

## Key Differences in Risks, Obligations, and Controls

### Risks and hazards are looked at differently

ISO 14001 asks the plant to identify environmental aspects and decide which have a significant impact. ISO 45001 asks for hazard identification and risk assessment focused on harm to people. A solvent can appear in both registers, but the questions differ: what happens if it reaches a drain, and what happens if a worker breathes it.

For the safety side, the [CCOHS guidance on risk assessment](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/hsprograms/hazard/risk_assessment.html) explains how to identify and rank workplace hazards in terms a supervisor can use.

### Legal obligations come from different places

Both standards require the plant to know and track its compliance obligations, but the sources differ. On the environmental side, an Ontario plant may need to follow the [Environmental Protection Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90e19) and hold the approvals that apply to its emissions, discharges and waste. On the safety side, the obligations come from occupational health and safety law and related regulations.

Neither standard makes a plant legally compliant by itself. Each requires a process for identifying obligations, evaluating compliance and acting when a gap appears.

### Worker participation is stronger in ISO 45001

ISO 45001 requires consultation with non-managerial workers and participation in areas such as hazard identification and incident investigation. ISO 14001 expects competent, aware people, but it does not carry the same formal expectation of worker participation. Plants that fold both standards into one system sometimes lose this requirement, because a single "awareness" training slide appears to cover everything. It does not. The guide on [how to meet ISO 45001 worker participation requirements](/blog/how-to-meet-iso-45001-worker-participation-requirements-in-6-steps) shows what the evidence looks like.

> **Important:** Treat worker consultation as its own requirement inside the integrated system. An auditor for ISO 45001 will look for records of how workers were involved, not only that they were trained.

### Controls and emergencies are different

Environmental controls usually address containment, treatment, monitoring and reduction of releases. Safety controls follow a hierarchy that favors eliminating the hazard first and uses personal protective equipment last. Emergency preparedness exists in both standards, but a spill drill and an injury response drill test different people and different equipment.

## How to Integrate Documentation, Objectives, and Audits

### Documentation

Start with a single document structure: one policy statement that covers both topics, one manual or system map, and shared procedures for document control, records, audits and corrective action. Keep separate registers where the content is different, namely the aspects and impacts register and the hazard and risk register.

### Objectives

Set objectives for each standard, then check that they do not compete. **Illustrative example:** A goal to cut compressed air use may reduce energy, but a change to ventilation could affect worker exposure. Reviewing objectives together catches that conflict before it reaches the shop floor.

### Audits

Integrated audit planning means the audit program lists both standards as criteria, assigns auditors who are competent in each, and schedules sampling so that environmental and safety processes are both tested. The audit guidelines in ISO 19011 support this approach, and the current edition is ISO 19011:2026.

A practical audit schedule looks like this:

1. Plan one audit cycle that names both standards as criteria.
2. Assign an auditor, or a pair of auditors, with competence in each topic.
3. Sample shared processes once and note the finding against both standards where it applies.
4. Sample the topic-specific requirements separately, such as significant aspects and worker consultation.
5. Report findings by standard so each corrective action has a clear owner.

> **Did You Know?** A certification body can often audit more than one management system standard in a single visit when the organization has integrated its system, but each standard is still assessed against its own requirements.

## When to Implement the Standards Together or Separately

A plant with a mature audit program and a single EHS owner will often integrate; a plant with an urgent injury trend or an open regulator inquiry will often stage.

### Reasons to implement together

- **Shared systems already exist.** A plant with working document control, internal audits and management review can extend them rather than build new ones.
- **One project team can carry both.** When the same person owns environmental and safety duties, as often happens in smaller shops, a joint project avoids duplicate meetings.
- **Customers or tenders ask for both.** If purchasing questionnaires ask for both certificates, a combined timeline avoids two separate certification efforts.

### Reasons to implement separately

- **One risk is clearly more urgent.** A plant with a recent injury trend or a regulator's attention on emissions may need to focus on one standard first.
- **Resources are thin.** A joint project asks a small team to learn two sets of requirements at once, and that can slow both.
- **The two functions are far apart.** When environmental and safety duties sit with separate managers who do not work together, a staged rollout lets each build ownership.

The [six-stage certification process](/process) applies to either path. The difference is whether the stages run once for both standards or twice in sequence.

## A Decision Framework for Ontario Manufacturers

The following five questions can help frame the choice.

1. **What is driving the project?** A customer or tender requirement points toward the standard named; a regulator or insurer concern points toward the topic under pressure.
2. **Which obligations are least controlled today?** Start with the standard that addresses the biggest known gap in compliance.
3. **Who will own the work?** If one person owns both topics, integrate. If two people own them, agree on shared processes first.
4. **What systems already exist?** The more documentation, audit and review practice is in place, the easier a joint rollout becomes.
5. **How much time can the plant give?** A staged approach spreads the effort, while a joint approach concentrates it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a manufacturer certify to ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 at the same time?

Yes. Many certification bodies offer combined audits, and a plant can pursue both certificates in one project. Each standard is still assessed against its own requirements, so the plant needs separate evidence for environmental aspects and for worker hazards.

### What processes can an integrated management system share?

Document control, records, internal audit, management review, corrective action, and competence and training are the usual shared processes. Risk registers, legal obligation registers and emergency plans are better kept topic-specific, even if they sit in the same system.

### Does ISO 14001 cover worker health and safety?

No. ISO 14001 addresses environmental aspects and impacts. A plant may control a chemical for both reasons, but worker health and safety management, including worker consultation, belongs to ISO 45001.

### How does an integrated audit assess both standards?

The audit plan lists both standards as criteria. The auditors sample shared processes once, then sample environmental and safety requirements separately. Findings are recorded against the standard they affect, so each corrective action is clear.

### Should a manufacturer implement ISO 14001 or ISO 45001 first?

It depends on what drives the project. A plant facing customer requests for one certificate, a pattern of injuries or environmental regulatory pressure should start with the matching standard. A plant with no clear pressure can start with the standard that fits its strongest existing processes.

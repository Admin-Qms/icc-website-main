> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Clause 5.4 of ISO 45001:2018 separates **consultation** (workers are asked and their views are weighed) from **participation** (workers take part in the decision itself), and an auditor will look for both.
> - The standard puts the emphasis on non-managerial workers, so a system that only involves supervisors will leave a gap.
> - Employers must provide the time, training, information and resources that make involvement possible, and must remove the barriers that get in its way.
> - Records matter: a consultation that left no trace, and no visible response, is hard to prove at audit.
> - A joint health and safety committee helps, but the standard does not require one on its own.

**Illustrative example:** A plant may reduce worker participation to a meeting schedule: a monthly safety committee meets, minutes are filed, and the plant assumes the box is ticked. Meeting ISO 45001 worker participation requirements takes more than that: operators, maintenance staff and temporary workers need real routes to shape decisions about their own safety, and the plant needs evidence that their input changed something.

The six steps below follow the structure of clause 5.4 and show what to do, what you need and a mistake that can trip up a plant. For the wider certification picture, [the plant manager's playbook for ISO 45001 certification in Ontario](/blog/the-plant-managers-playbook-for-iso-45001-certification-in-ontario) covers the full path from gap analysis to registrar audit.

## Step 1: Define Who Participates and What Is in Scope

Start by listing every group of people who work under the plant's control. That includes line operators, setup technicians, maintenance crews, shipping and receiving staff, office workers who walk the floor, and agency or contract workers who perform tasks on site. ISO 45001:2018 asks organizations to involve workers at all levels and functions, and it specifically points to non-managerial workers as the group that must not be overlooked.

![a group of press operators and a maintenance technician reviewing a lockout tag at the side of a stamping press line](/images/blog/how-to-meet-iso-45001-worker-participation-requirements-in-6-steps/inline-1.webp)

**What you need:** a current headcount by department and shift, a list of contractors who work on site, and a short statement of scope that names who is covered.

**Common mistake:** treating team leads as the voice of the floor. A leader who also controls overtime and discipline is not always the person an operator will speak candidly to, so build in routes that bypass the supervisor.

## Step 2: Identify Decisions Where Worker Input Is Needed

Clause 5.4 of ISO 45001:2018 lists the decisions where the organization must consult workers and the decisions where it must involve them directly. The lists are different, and the difference is worth building into a simple matrix.

The standard calls for consultation on matters such as:

- Determining the needs and expectations of interested parties, so the plant understands what workers and others reasonably expect.
- Establishing the OH&S policy and the objectives that follow from it.
- Assigning organizational roles, responsibilities and authorities for safety.
- Deciding how to meet legal and other requirements, and how to control outsourcing, procurement and contractors.
- Planning the audit program and driving continual improvement.

It calls for participation, a stronger form of involvement, in matters such as:

- Setting the mechanisms for consultation and participation themselves.
- Hazard identification and risk and opportunity assessment.
- Choosing actions to eliminate hazards and reduce risks.
- Deciding competence needs, training and how the training is evaluated.
- Determining what needs to be monitored and measured.
- Investigating incidents and nonconformities and choosing corrective actions.

> **Key Consideration:** Build the matrix as one page, with each decision on a row and the forum or method on the same row. When an auditor asks how workers shaped the policy, the plant can point to a row rather than reconstruct the answer from memory.

**What you need:** a copy of clause 5.4, the plant's list of safety decisions over the past year, and a column for how each one will involve workers.

**Common mistake:** consulting on the easy items, such as new gloves, and skipping the harder ones, such as risk ratings or contractor controls.

## Step 3: Set Up Practical Consultation Channels

A channel works only if people use it, so match each one to how the shop actually runs. Many plants combine several methods: a toolbox talk at the start of a shift, a suggestion board near the time clock, a short paper form, a named worker representative per department and a joint health and safety committee. No single method is mandated by ISO 45001, so choose what suits the people on the floor.

Ontario employers already have duties here. The [Occupational Health and Safety Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90o01) sets out the legal responsibilities of employers, supervisors and workers, and depending on workplace size it requires a joint health and safety committee or a health and safety representative. The province's [guide to health and safety committees and representatives](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-health-and-safety-committees-and-representatives) explains how those bodies are formed and how they operate. A plant that already has a working committee can use it as one channel in the system, though it should not be the only one. Workers who do not sit on the committee still need a way to be heard.

![a safety suggestion board with handwritten cards mounted on a wall beside a CNC machining cell](/images/blog/how-to-meet-iso-45001-worker-participation-requirements-in-6-steps/inline-2.webp)

**Illustrative example:** A machine shop with two shifts finds that the day shift attends the monthly committee meeting while the night shift never does. The plant adds a five-minute huddle at the start of the night shift, a rotating night-shift representative and a locked suggestion box that the safety coordinator empties every week. Night-shift concerns now reach the same log as the day-shift items.

**What you need:** a list of channels, who owns each one, and a rule for how often each is reviewed.

**Common mistake:** relying on a single channel that suits office hours and misses nights, weekends and temporary workers.

## Step 4: Provide Time, Information, and Training

Clause 5.4 expects the organization to provide the mechanisms, time, training and resources that consultation and participation need. In practice, that means paid time on the clock to attend meetings, join risk assessments and sit in on incident reviews. If a worker has to do this on a break or after the shift, participation will be thin and uneven.

Workers also need timely access to clear, understandable and relevant information about the OH&S management system. Post the policy, the hazard register summaries and the results of audits where people can see them, and write them in plain words. Where the workforce speaks several languages, translate the items that matter most, such as emergency procedures and reporting routes.

Training belongs in this step as well. Workers who sit on a committee or join a risk assessment need to know how hazards are rated and what the plant's controls hierarchy looks like. The [Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety's guidance on risk assessment](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/hsprograms/hazard/risk_assessment.html) is a practical reference for explaining how hazards are identified and ranked.

> **Important:** Clause 5.4 of ISO 45001:2018 names time, training and resources as things the organization must provide for consultation and participation. Keep attendance records, and note that the time was paid.

**What you need:** a budget line for paid participation time, a one-page summary of the system written for the floor, and a short training outline for representatives.

**Common mistake:** giving workers a seat at the table without the information to contribute. A representative who has never seen the hazard register cannot challenge it.

## Step 5: Use Worker Input in Hazard Identification and Risk Controls

This step is where participation shows up in the work itself. Workers know which guard gets bypassed to hit a rate, which aisle floods when it rains and which task has no safe place to stand. The standard asks the plant to involve them in hazard identification and risk assessment because that knowledge is hard to get any other way.

Put workers into the routines you already run:

1. Include an operator from the area in each job hazard analysis, and have that person sign off on the draft.
2. Ask workers who do the task to walk through new equipment or layout changes before they are approved.
3. Invite affected workers to incident and near-miss reviews, and let them propose corrective actions.
4. Give every worker a simple, non-punitive way to report a hazard, with a promise that reports will be answered.

Incident investigations deserve particular attention. The standard lists the investigation of incidents and nonconformities among the matters where workers participate, so a plant that investigates behind closed doors has a gap. For a closer look at how auditors test this area, see the [ISO 45001 internal audit checklist for manufacturing plants](/blog/10-point-iso-45001-internal-audit-checklist-for-manufacturing-plants).

**What you need:** a hazard reporting form or app, a standing agenda item for worker findings, and a rule that every report gets a reply within a set time.

**Common mistake:** collecting hazard reports and never closing the loop. Workers stop reporting when nothing visibly changes.

## Step 6: Record Feedback, Respond, and Review Effectiveness

Participation only counts at audit if it can be shown. Keep a single log where every piece of worker input is recorded, with the date, the channel it arrived through, the decision taken and the reply given. A spreadsheet is enough for a small shop, and a [QMS platform](/platform) can hold the same records with reminders and owners attached.

The log should cover several kinds of evidence:

- Committee minutes and attendance lists that show which workers took part.
- Hazard reports and the corrective actions they led to, with closure dates.
- Records of consultation on policy, objectives and contractor controls.
- Notes from toolbox talks or huddles that show topics raised and actions agreed.

Then review whether the system works. Management review under ISO 45001:2018 should include the results of consultation and participation, and the plant should ask a few direct questions. Are all shifts represented? Do workers know how to report a hazard? Are replies prompt? A short anonymous survey once a year can help, as long as the plant acts on the results and shares what changed.

Barriers deserve a standing place in this review. Common obstacles include language differences, fear of reprisal, shift patterns that exclude some workers, low literacy, and a culture where raising a concern is seen as complaining. Clause 5.4 expects the plant to identify and remove these barriers, and to reduce the ones it cannot remove entirely.

> **Did You Know?** Clause 5.4 of ISO 45001:2018 includes identifying and removing obstacles or barriers to participation, and minimizing those that cannot be removed.

**What you need:** a feedback log, a review date on the management review calendar, and a named owner for removing each barrier.

**Common mistake:** recording the meeting but not the response. A log of suggestions with no outcomes shows that the plant listened, not that it acted.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does ISO 45001 define worker participation?

Worker participation means involving workers directly in the decision-making of the OH&S management system, as distinct from consultation, where their views are sought before the organization decides. Clause 5.4 of ISO 45001:2018 lists the matters that call for each. Participation applies to areas such as hazard identification, risk assessment, incident investigation and the choice of controls.

### Does ISO 45001 require a health and safety committee?

The standard does not require a committee as such. It requires the plant to set up mechanisms for consultation and participation, and a committee is one common way to do it. Ontario plants may also have a separate legal duty under the Occupational Health and Safety Act to maintain a joint committee or a representative, depending on the size and nature of the workplace.

### How can a manufacturer document worker consultation?

Keep a log that records the topic, the date, the people consulted, the channel used, the decision taken and the reply given. Add meeting minutes, attendance lists and hazard reports as supporting evidence. Link each entry to the management system decision it influenced, such as a revised procedure or a new control.

### Can workers participate in incident investigations under ISO 45001?

Yes, and the standard expects it. Clause 5.4 of ISO 45001:2018 includes the investigation of incidents and nonconformities, and the determination of corrective actions, among the matters in which workers participate. A plant can involve the affected worker, a representative from the area and a health and safety representative, with the aim of finding causes rather than assigning blame.

### What barriers to worker participation should an employer address?

Employers should look for language differences, fear of reprisal, shift schedules that exclude some workers, limited access to information and a culture that discourages speaking up. Time, training and clear replies to every report also matter. The standard expects the plant to identify these obstacles and remove or reduce them.

Plants that treat participation as a working part of their safety system, rather than a meeting to be minuted, usually find the audit conversation easier and the floor more engaged.

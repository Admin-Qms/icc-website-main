Processors and packagers integrating ISO 22000, HACCP and CFIA requirements into one auditable system.

Talk to an industry specialist Free readiness check

Food and beverage companies in Canada typically certify to ISO 22000 or to FSSC 22000, the GFSI-recognized scheme built on it, because retail and food-service customers ask for a recognized food safety certificate. Many add ISO 9001 for quality and ISO 14001 for environmental management. None of them replaces the licensing and preventive control requirements of the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations.

The pressure comes from two directions at once. A business that manufactures, processes or packages food for interprovincial trade, or imports food, generally needs a license and a written preventive control plan showing how biological, chemical and physical hazards are identified and controlled. Customers then audit against their own requirements, and an allergen or labeling error can mean a recall in which you must prove which lots are affected.

ISO 22000:2018, with Amendment 1:2024, is the current food safety standard. FSSC 22000 Version 7 was released in May 2026. ISO 9001:2026 and ISO 14001:2026 were both published in 2026, and many certified organizations remain on the 2015 editions, whose certificates stay valid during the transition.

Which food safety certification does a Canadian food processor need?

Start with what your customers ask for. If a retailer or brand owner requires a GFSI-recognized certificate, ISO 22000 on its own will not meet that requirement: FSSC 22000 appears on the GFSI list of recognized programs, while ISO 22000 alone does not. Because FSSC 22000 is built on ISO 22000, a system designed to ISO 22000 is a natural first step toward it.

Regulatory compliance runs alongside certification, not through it. A certificate records a certification body's view of your system. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency assesses your license and preventive control plan on its own terms.

  • ISO 22000:2018

    The certifiable food safety management system standard, combining prerequisite programs, hazard analysis and control based on HACCP principles, and the management system requirements shared with other ISO standards.

  • FSSC 22000 Version 7

    A GFSI-recognized certification scheme that adds sector prerequisite program standards and its own additional requirements to ISO 22000, and the one to choose when a customer names a GFSI benchmark.

  • Preventive control plan under the SFCR

    A legal requirement for many federal license holders, written to show how hazards are identified and controlled and how labeling, grade and standard of identity requirements are met.

  • ISO 9001

    Useful where customers audit specification compliance, complaint handling and supplier control beyond food safety, for example at co-packers and private-label producers.

  • ISO 14001

    Brings wastewater, refrigerants, packaging waste and energy use under control, which matters when customers run sustainability programs or municipal discharge limits apply.

What do food safety auditors look for in a processing plant?

Auditors follow the hazard analysis into the plant. They pick a critical control point and ask for the monitoring records, who signed them, what happened when a reading fell outside the critical limit, and who verified the corrective action. A plan not reflected in the daily records quickly becomes a major finding.

Expect questions on allergen control during changeovers, sanitation verification, approved suppliers with current certificates, and traceability one step forward and one step back. ISO 22000:2018 requires the withdrawal and recall process to be verified, so a mock recall with the time taken and the lots found is standard evidence.

Prerequisite program records carry as much weight as the plan. Pest control reports, environmental monitoring results, checks on metal detectors and thermometers, and training records for line staff are all sampled, and auditors look for trends that someone reviewed, not reports that were only filed.

Illustrative example: A bakery supplying private-label bread runs a mock recall on a flour lot. The receiving records name the lot, but the production log only records the date, so the plant cannot narrow the affected product below a full day of output. The fix is recording the ingredient lot on the batch sheet, not rewriting the recall procedure.

How do you combine ISO 22000 with ISO 9001 and ISO 14001?

ISO management system standards share a harmonized structure: the same clause numbers and titles, common terms and definitions, and identical core text. That lets one document control process, one internal audit program and one management review serve food safety, quality and the environment, while the hazard control plan and prerequisite programs stay specific to food safety.

Keep the food safety team at the center. ISO 22000:2018 expects the team to bring multidisciplinary knowledge and experience, and the same team is well placed to review changes to recipes, suppliers, equipment and packaging before they reach the line, with environmental and quality effects assessed in the same review.

A practical start is a gap analysis that sets your preventive control plan and current records against the standard you are targeting, followed by a decision on scope: which sites, product lines and storage facilities the certificate will cover. Anything left out of scope cannot appear on the certificate, so check what your customers expect to see named. For a single plant with a working preventive control plan, certification typically takes six to twelve months. The readiness check gives you a first view of where you stand.

Food & Beverage questions, answered

Does ISO 22000 certification replace a CFIA preventive control plan?

No. The preventive control plan is a legal requirement under the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations for many license holders, and ISO 22000 certification does not substitute for it. Both rest on hazard analysis and control measures, so one food safety management system can hold the plan, its records and its verification. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency still assesses the plan against the regulations.

Is ISO 22000 or FSSC 22000 better for a Canadian food manufacturer?

It depends on who buys your product. FSSC 22000 is recognized by GFSI and ISO 22000 alone is not, so if a retailer or brand owner requires a GFSI-recognized certificate, FSSC 22000 is the one to pursue. If customers only ask for a certified food safety management system, ISO 22000 is sufficient, and because FSSC 22000 is built on it, upgrading later does not mean starting over.

How long does food safety certification take for a single plant?

For a single plant that already holds a license and runs a working preventive control plan, certification typically takes six to twelve months. The time goes into closing prerequisite program gaps, building records that show controls operate every shift, internal audits, a management review and the two-stage certification audit. A plant starting without documented prerequisite programs should expect the longer end of that range.

Do food companies need ISO 9001 as well as a food safety certificate?

Not always. A food safety certificate covers hazards to the consumer, while ISO 9001 covers whether you consistently meet customer and specification requirements. Co-packers, ingredient suppliers and private-label producers often find customers audit both. ISO 9001 can be added to an existing ISO 22000 system, sharing its document control, internal audits and management review.

Which version of FSSC 22000 applies in 2026?

FSSC 22000 Version 7 was released on 1 May 2026, and audits against Version 6 are permitted until 30 April 2027. A plant certifying for the first time should build to Version 7. A plant certified to Version 6 should plan its upgrade with its certification body well before that date.

ISO consulting for Construction

Contractors and fabricators winning tenders with integrated quality, safety and environmental management.

Talk to an industry specialist Free readiness check

Construction companies in Canada typically pursue ISO 9001 for quality, ISO 45001 for health and safety and ISO 14001 for environmental management, because public owners, general contractors and infrastructure tenders ask for certified systems at prequalification. In Ontario, safety prequalification also turns on COR, which the Infrastructure Health & Safety Association grants in the province.

The difficulty in construction is that the workplace keeps changing. Each project brings a new site, a new owner specification, new subcontractors and a crew that may not have worked together before. Quality problems show up as rework and deficiency lists, safety obligations fall on the constructor under provincial law, and dust, sediment runoff, noise and waste have to be controlled at every site.

On editions: ISO 45001:2018 is current. ISO 9001:2026 and ISO 14001:2026 were both published in 2026, and many certified organizations remain on the 2015 editions, whose certificates stay valid during the transition periods that end in 2029.

Do construction companies need ISO 45001 or COR to win tenders?

Read the tender, because the answer changes from owner to owner. IHSA describes COR as often required for public and private sector construction projects in Ontario. IHSA stopped issuing COR 2020 equivalency for ISO 45001:2018 certificates on March 3, 2025, so an ISO 45001 certificate is no longer converted into a COR equivalency in the province.

Some owners now accept either. Since March 31, 2025, the City of Toronto has required an occupational health and safety management system certificate at the qualification stage of every construction contract, and it accepts IHSA COR 2020, the provincial Supporting Ontario Safe Employers (SOSE) certificate or ISO 45001:2018 from an accredited certification body.

  • ISO 9001

    Shows owners that inspection and test plans, hold points, nonconforming work and subcontractor control are managed consistently on every project.

  • ISO 45001:2018

    An international health and safety management system standard that suits contractors working in several provinces or for owners who name it; ISO 45001 certification is audited by an accredited certification body.

  • COR

    A nationally trademarked safety program that IHSA grants in Ontario, audited against its own elements and often listed as a bidding requirement on its own.

  • ISO 14001

    Addresses environmental aspects at each site, from spill response and sediment control to waste and excess soil, together with the permits and bylaws that apply.

What do auditors look for on a construction project?

Certification auditors want to see the system working on live projects, so expect a site visit. Under ISO 9001:2015 they test how subcontractors and suppliers are selected and evaluated, whether the drawings on site are the current revision, and how deficiencies are recorded and closed. Under ISO 45001:2018 they look for site-specific hazard assessments, worker participation and the coordination of subcontractors working side by side. Toolbox talk records and site inspection reports show whether the safety program reaches the people doing the work.

Illustrative example: A general contractor with three active sites has a strong corporate quality manual. At the second site, the auditor finds formwork installed to a drawing superseded by a revision issued two weeks earlier, and no record of who received it. The finding lands on document control at the site trailer, not on the manual.

Environmental controls are checked the same way. Under ISO 14001:2015 an auditor may walk the site to see sediment controls, fuel storage and spill kits, then ask for the inspection records behind them and the evaluation of compliance with the permits and bylaws that apply to that project.

How do you set up one system across head office and every job site?

ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 share a harmonized structure: the same clause numbers and titles, common terms and definitions, and identical core text. A contractor can run one integrated system in which head office owns the procedures, internal audits, corrective action and management review, and each project adds a short project plan covering its quality checks, hazards and environmental controls.

Subcontractor control is where the integrated system pays off, and a project plan short enough for a site superintendent to use every day does more than a binder that stays in the office. One onboarding step can confirm a subcontractor's safety credentials, insurance, training records and quality expectations, and one evaluation after the job can feed both the approved subcontractor list and the next tender.

Start with a gap analysis against the standard the tenders name, then decide the scope: which services, regions and types of project the certificate will cover. Plan the certification audit for a time when at least one representative project is active. For a contractor with an existing safety program, certification typically takes six to twelve months. Construction questions, answered

Does ISO 45001 replace COR in Ontario?

Not automatically. IHSA stopped issuing COR 2020 equivalency for ISO 45001:2018 certificates on March 3, 2025, so the two are now separate credentials. Some owners accept either one; the City of Toronto, for example, accepts IHSA COR 2020, the SOSE certificate or ISO 45001:2018 for construction contracts. Other owners name COR specifically. Check each tender before deciding which to pursue.

Which ISO certification does a contractor need for government construction tenders in Canada?

It depends on the owner and the province. ISO 9001 is the standard for quality management, and health and safety requirements are met through COR, ISO 45001 or a provincial program, depending on what the tender accepts. Infrastructure and environmental work may also ask for ISO 14001. Read the prequalification section of each tender, and keep a list of what your target owners require.

Does a general contractor's ISO certificate cover its subcontractors?

No. A certificate covers the certified organization and the scope named on it. Subcontractors are controlled through the contractor's own system: how they are selected and evaluated, what is checked when they arrive on site, and how their work is inspected. An owner may set its own requirements for subcontractors, so the contract documents decide whether a subcontractor also needs its own certificate.

How long does ISO 9001 certification take for a construction company?

For a contractor with an established way of running projects, ISO 9001 certification typically takes six to twelve months. Much of the time goes into writing a consistent project quality process, building records on live projects, running internal audits and a management review, then completing the two-stage certification audit. The schedule also depends on having an active project for the auditor to visit.

Should a construction company certify to ISO 9001:2015 or ISO 9001:2026?

ISO 9001:2026 was published on 16 September 2026, and certificates to ISO 9001:2015 remain valid during a three-year transition ending in September 2029. A contractor building a new system can design it to the 2026 edition. One already certified to the 2015 edition keeps a valid certificate and can plan its transition with its certification body.

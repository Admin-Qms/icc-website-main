> **Key Takeaways**
> - Auditor competence combines knowledge of requirements, automotive processes and the ability to gather objective evidence.
> - Organizations should define the skills needed for each audit assignment and assess people against those needs.
> - Training records help show development, but course attendance alone does not establish practical competence.
> - Maintain evidence of assessment, audit activity and ongoing development, and check current applicable customer and scheme requirements.

An internal audit can follow a part from an incoming shipment to final release, but the value of that audit depends on the person asking the questions. IATF 16949 internal auditor competency requirements matter because an automotive supplier needs auditors who can connect requirements, process controls and evidence rather than simply complete a checklist.

The task is broader than knowing the title of a standard. An auditor needs to understand the assigned process, recognize the criteria being used and assess whether a record or observation supports a conclusion. For an Ontario supplier, that can mean examining how a customer requirement reaches a control plan or how a change is reviewed before production.

> **Key Consideration:** Define competence for the audit being assigned. An auditor's general experience is useful, but it does not automatically demonstrate knowledge of every process or customer requirement.

## Why auditor competence matters on the shop floor

IATF 16949:2016 is the automotive quality management system standard used alongside ISO 9001. The current ISO quality management edition in the publisher's standards table is ISO 9001:2026; many certified organizations remain on the 2015 edition during the transition. A supplier should verify its applicable certification arrangements, customer requirements and transition instructions before deciding which criteria apply to an audit.

[IATF's description of IATF 16949:2016](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/iatf-169492016/about/) explains the automotive quality management system standard and its relationship with ISO 9001. The supplier's own documented system, applicable customer-specific requirements and current IATF publications also form part of the context for a particular assignment.

An internal auditor does not need to operate every machine, but must understand enough about the process to recognize the right evidence and ask useful follow-up questions. In a receiving audit, for instance, the issue may be how the supplier verifies purchased material and communicates a hold. In a production audit, the question may concern the relationship between the approved control plan and the checks performed at the workstation.

**Competence is demonstrated in the context of assigned work.** A person who can audit document control may need additional preparation before auditing a specialized manufacturing process. An assessment should identify that gap before the audit starts, rather than rely on the final report to reveal it.

## Build the technical foundation

Begin by identifying the criteria an auditor must apply. The list may include applicable ISO 9001 and IATF 16949 requirements, the organization's own procedures, customer-specific requirements and process documents. Auditors should understand how to find the current applicable material and distinguish a requirement from a local practice.

Knowing where a requirement is recorded is not the same as understanding how it applies. An auditor should be able to connect a criterion to a process, select evidence that addresses it and identify when more information is needed. When a requirement is unclear, the auditor should consult the approved source or a competent technical resource rather than guess.

Automotive processes may use tools such as process flow diagrams, failure mode and effects analysis, control plans, measurement system analysis and statistical process control. The [AIAG quality resources](https://www.aiag.org/expertise-areas/quality) describe automotive quality initiatives and core tools. Which tools matter to an assignment depends on the plant's work and the applicable customer requirements.

**A control plan is a starting point for a question, not proof that a control happened.** The auditor can compare the planned control with the workstation instruction, a relevant record and an observation of the process. The [control plan overview](/blog/what-iatf-16949-control-plan-requirements-mean-for-automotive-suppliers) explains that document's role in automotive production.

A practical knowledge assessment can ask the prospective auditor to explain how information moves between related records. It can also ask the person to identify what evidence would be needed to assess a change. These activities reveal whether the person can apply the material, rather than merely recognize terminology.

## Develop automotive audit skills

A sound audit starts with a defined scope and criteria. The auditor plans which processes and interfaces to follow, identifies people to speak with and selects records or activities that can provide relevant evidence. Sampling has limits: an auditor should be clear about what was examined and avoid claiming that a sample proves every item is correct.

At the workstation, observation and discussion should be connected to the process being assessed. Ask how an operator identifies the current instruction, what happens when a result is outside the specified criterion and who can authorize the next step. Compare the explanation with the applicable document and a sample of records, then investigate any apparent difference before reaching a conclusion.

The following skills are useful across assignments:

- **Plan a process trail.** Identify the input, activity, output and handoff that need to be examined against the audit criteria.
- **Interview without leading.** Ask the person responsible to explain the work, then clarify how the applicable instruction is found and used.
- **Evaluate objective evidence.** Record the relevant observation or record and check that it actually addresses the audit question.
- **Write a clear finding.** Connect the criterion, observed evidence and identified gap without assigning blame or overstating the sample.
- **Escalate appropriately.** Use the plant's established process when an observation may affect product or customer requirements.

**Illustrative example:** An unnamed automotive supplier assigns a trainee to observe an experienced auditor reviewing a production change. The trainee compares the approved change record with a control plan and explains the next evidence needed. The supervising auditor assesses the explanation and records any development needed before an independent assignment.

The example is hypothetical. An actual supplier should define how supervision, independence and approval are handled within its own audit program. The important distinction is between exposure to an audit and demonstrated ability to perform the assigned work.

> **Important:** A potential product issue should enter the plant's normal containment and escalation process without waiting for the final audit report.

## Choose training and qualification pathways

Training can introduce requirements, audit methods and automotive tools. A useful plan begins with a gap: what the auditor will be asked to assess, what knowledge is already demonstrated and where additional development is needed. The organization can then choose a suitable combination of instruction, guided practice and assessment.

Course selection should be based on current applicable requirements and the scope of the auditor's work. A certificate may document attendance or completion, but it does not automatically prove that the person can apply the material at a particular plant. Where a customer or certification arrangement specifies additional conditions, the supplier should verify those conditions from the current authoritative source.

The [IATF Global Oversight site](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/) is a source for official IATF publications and information. A supplier should check current sanctioned interpretations and applicable customer-specific requirements rather than rely on an old training slide when defining its competence criteria.

A training plan should answer several practical questions:

- What audit criteria and customer requirements must the person understand for the assignment?
- Which manufacturing processes and relevant automotive tools will the person examine?
- How will the organization assess practical application, not just attendance?
- Who can authorize the person for a defined audit scope?
- What development or reassessment is needed when requirements or assignments change?

The [IATF 16949 service overview](/services/iatf-16949) explains the broader automotive quality system context. Training decisions remain the supplier's responsibility and should be based on its actual obligations, not a promise that one course qualifies every auditor for every assignment.

## Assess and document competence

A documented competence assessment should show the criteria used, the evidence reviewed, the assessor's conclusion and any restrictions or development actions. Evidence might include relevant education, experience, training, an observed audit, a review of findings or another assessment appropriate to the role. The record should make clear what the person is authorized to audit and who made that decision.

A matrix can help a coordinator see which auditors are available for particular processes. Its value depends on the evidence behind it and on whether it is updated when responsibilities or requirements change. A checkmark with no supporting assessment makes it difficult to distinguish a completed course from demonstrated ability.

**Keep the assessment tied to the planned audit scope.** If a person is competent to audit one manufacturing process, the organization should consider whether an assignment in another process requires additional knowledge or support. The assessment can identify a mentor, technical specialist or development step where appropriate.

Objectivity also matters when selecting the audit team. A quality manager may have relevant technical knowledge, but the organization must arrange assignments so the audit remains impartial and does not become a review of the auditor's own work. Consider responsibility and reporting relationships before the schedule is issued.

The audit report and subsequent follow-up provide another opportunity to evaluate performance. Were findings supported by objective evidence? Did the auditor identify the applicable criterion and communicate the conclusion accurately? The answers can inform coaching without turning a single observation into an unsupported conclusion about the person's overall ability.

> **Key Consideration:** A qualification record should be easy to retrieve, but its purpose is to support a sound assignment decision, not simply satisfy a document request.

## Maintain competence as requirements and processes change

Competence is not a one-time event. An auditor who has not examined a process recently may need a refresher on its current controls; a new customer requirement or manufacturing change may call for additional preparation. The organization should follow applicable IATF and customer requirements for maintaining competence and verify the current details from authoritative sources.

A development review can consider audit activity, changes to applicable criteria, feedback on audit performance and the knowledge needed for upcoming assignments. It should distinguish a requirement to maintain competence from an assumption that every person must take the same course on the same schedule. Where a fixed interval or minimum activity applies, confirm it in the current applicable IATF requirements and interpretations.

Common gaps include assigning an auditor to an unfamiliar process without support, keeping training records without assessment, and failing to update competence records after a change. Another is treating internal audits and certification-body audits as interchangeable: they have different purposes and may have different qualification arrangements.

The [manufacturing industry overview](/industries/manufacturing) provides context for the variety of operations an audit program may need to cover. The plant should evaluate its own process complexity and customer obligations when deciding how to build the audit team.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What formal qualifications does an internal auditor need in Canada?

The organization should determine and document auditor competence against the applicable IATF 16949 requirements, customer-specific requirements and the audit assignment. Do not assume a particular external certificate is universally sufficient or mandatory without checking the current authoritative requirements that apply to the supplier.

### Does an internal auditor need ISO 9001 knowledge before IATF training?

An auditor needs the knowledge required to assess the applicable criteria, including the relationship between the automotive standard and ISO 9001. The organization should assess the person's existing foundation and address any gaps as part of its development plan.

### How often must competence be refreshed?

Check the current applicable IATF requirements, sanctioned interpretations and customer requirements, then follow the organization's competence process. Changes in requirements, audit activity or assigned processes can also indicate a need for development or reassessment.

### Can a quality manager be an internal auditor without an external certificate?

A quality manager can be considered for an assignment if the organization demonstrates the required competence and preserves objectivity and impartiality. An external certificate alone does not resolve either question. Any applicable customer-specific or scheme conditions must also be checked.

### What documentation can demonstrate competence?

Relevant records may include the competence criteria, training and experience, assessment results, authorization scope, audit activity and development actions. The organization should retain the documented information required by its applicable system and make the evidence available for review.

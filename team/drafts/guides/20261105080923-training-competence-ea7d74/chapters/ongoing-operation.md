## Review decision quality and improve

Once the workflow is live, the question changes from "does it work?" to "is it still producing good decisions?" A periodic review answers it. The review looks at the decisions supervisors make, the exceptions that arise, and what internal audits and corrective actions reveal. Individual qualification renewal belongs to the earlier chapter; this review looks at the workflow as a whole.

The Auditing Practices Group, in its [guidance on auditing competence](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-Competence.pdf), notes that internal audit results can show whether the people doing the work have the competence they need, and that corrective actions often identify training needs. Those are useful inputs to a review. Ask what recent findings say about competence and whether the matrix and the evidence rules reflect them. The guidance was written for ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.2 auditors and is not an additional standard.

### Sample decisions, not just records

Choose a sample of recent staffing decisions, for example, ten assignments across several tasks and shifts, plus every exception recorded since the last review. For each, ask four questions.

1. Which person was assigned, and what evidence supported it?
2. Was the evidence consistent with the agreed rules for that task?
3. If evidence was missing, what happened, and who decided?
4. Did the outcome match what the task required?

Compare what you find with locally established measures. These might include how long it takes to retrieve evidence for a sampled person, how many gaps remain open, and how many assignments needed an exception. Define a baseline and the collection method before comparing, so that the numbers mean the same thing from one review to the next. Do not invent return-on-investment figures or claim that the software guarantees compliance. Neither the supplied sources nor the evidence gathered for this guide supports such claims.

**Illustrative example:** An unnamed Canadian manufacturer of machined components samples recent inspection assignments and investigates one with a missing assessment reference. The quality lead traces it to a record entered under an older task name. The fix is a correction to the record and a note in the migration exception list, and the owner and due date are recorded.

Look at the exceptions in particular. A pattern of the same exception, such as repeated missing assessments for one task, points to a cause to investigate: scarce assessors, criteria that are hard to apply, or learning scheduled too late. Also check whether exceptions are being identified and escalated at all, since a small number may mean few occurred or few were reported.

### Decide what to improve

The quality and operations owners evaluate the findings, prioritize improvements, and record the review decision, each action's owner, and the follow-up. The result is a short list of actions, each with an owner and a date. Distinguish the causes. Some findings point to training, some to a confusing rule, some to configuration, and some to a task definition that has drifted from the work. A change to a task definition goes back through the approval path from the matrix chapter. A change to a rule is documented and communicated. A configuration change is tested against the pilot scenarios before use.

If the evidence is too incomplete to assess decision quality, say so in the review record and treat the gap as the first finding. A review that finds nothing because nothing could be seen has not found that all is well.

### Keep the cadence realistic

Choose an interval that the team will keep, such as quarterly for the first year, and put it in the calendar with a named owner. Record each review, its findings, and the follow-up, so that the next one can start from the last.

## Answer common questions without overstating compliance

Teams and auditors ask similar questions when they evaluate competency management software. The answers below rely on the supplied sources and avoid claims they do not support. The [APG guidance](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-Competence.pdf) concerns ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.2, is not an additional standard, and does not establish the requirements of any later edition. The [ICC training and competency module page](https://isocertificationconsultants.ca/solutions/training-competence/) is a published product description. The quality lead reviews and records approved answers with their source references. Questions needing legal or standards interpretation beyond this evidence should go to a qualified adviser.

### What operational question should competency management software answer first?

The first question is the staffing decision: who may do this task, on this shift, and on what evidence? Software that cannot answer it for a named person and task, including what is still unknown, is not yet solving the problem. Write the question in plain words before comparing tools, then test every candidate against it with realistic records, including an incomplete one.

### How is training completion different from demonstrated competence?

Completion shows that a learning action took place. Competence is the ability to apply knowledge and skills to achieve intended results. The APG guidance states that participation in a course does not by itself prove a person is competent, because it may show only that knowledge was gained. A certificate or quiz score is useful evidence of completion, and the task's evidence rules decide whether anything more is needed, usually a practical assessment.

### When is a formal qualification required in addition to competence?

A formal qualification is required only where a statute, standard, customer, or other requirement calls for one. The APG guidance says qualifications are not always required, but competencies are, and that where a qualification is required, the organization should determine it and be able to demonstrate that it is maintained. It gives welders for specific welding conditions as an example. Read the scope and validity conditions from the requirement itself, and do not assume them.

### What evidence should support a competency matrix entry?

The entry should rest on the evidence the team agreed for that task and level: the approved task requirement, the action taken to acquire competence, the evaluation of its effectiveness, and any applicable qualification. The APG guidance says clause 7.2 d calls for appropriate documented information as evidence of competence and that the organization decides what is appropriate. Record who confirmed the entry and when, and mark entries whose evidence is out of date.

### How should a supervisor handle a missing assessment or an absent qualified worker?

Treat a missing practical assessment as awaiting assessment, not as approval, and do not assign independent work on that evidence. If the usual worker is absent, choose a replacement whose evidence supports the task, supervise only where applicable qualification conditions and local rules allow it, or hold the work. Escalate to the exception owner when none is available. Record the decision and the evidence consulted. The APG guidance notes that auditors may check that adequate replacements exist when the usual person is absent.

### Which legacy training records should be migrated, reviewed or retained separately?

Sort them by kind and reliability. Records with a clear source, a person, a task, a date, and a confirmer; a task requirement the team still recognizes; and, for assessments, criteria are candidates for migration once the migration lead approves the transfer. Plausible but incomplete records need review first. Records that cannot be tied to a person or task, or that conflict with others, stay outside the system with a label and must not support staffing decisions. A migration should never raise anyone's status. Confirm with the supplier what the system can represent safely.

### What might an auditor ask to see beyond training plans and records?

The [APG guidance](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-Competence.pdf) says the existence of training plans and records is not sufficient on its own to show how an organization ensures competence. Auditors may sample people, observe their work, interview them about the competence the task needs, and check that adequate replacements exist. Prepare people as well as files: a supervisor should be able to explain how coverage is chosen, and an operator should be able to say what the task requires and what they have demonstrated.

### Does ICC's standards mapping mean the software guarantees conformity or certification?

No. The [ICC module page](https://isocertificationconsultants.ca/solutions/training-competence/) maps the module to ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.2, ISO 13485:2016 clause 6.2, and ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clause 6.2. A mapping shows where the records may be relevant. It does not state that software alone satisfies a clause, and it does not promise an audit result. Conformity depends on how the organization defines competence, acts on gaps, and keeps appropriate evidence. ISO 10015:2019 offers useful guidance on competence management, but the [APG guidance](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-Competence.pdf) says it is not a required standard and should not be used as audit criteria in an ISO 9001:2015 audit.

### What should the team ask ICC to demonstrate and confirm in writing?

Ask for a live demonstration of the staffing decision and its exception using the team's scenario, and separate what is shown from what is only described. Ask for written answers on integrations with human resources systems, pricing and trials, which the supplied page does not describe, and confirm access controls, support, and data export as well. Record the answers in the results sheet and treat an unanswered question as an open condition.

## Prepare a focused ICC demonstration

The demonstration may serve a first purchasing decision or reassess a live system; either way, it is most useful when the team brings the problem. The [published ICC module description](https://isocertificationconsultants.ca/solutions/training-competence/) lists what the supplier says the module does; the demonstration is where the team checks that against its own workflow, using the evidence rules in the [APG guidance](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-Competence.pdf) as background rather than as a checklist. Send the scenario ahead and ask the supplier to work through it, in order, rather than present a general tour.

Decide in advance who will attend from the plant. A supervisor, an assessor, and a quality reviewer each notice different things, and an IT representative can ask about access and data. Ask the supplier to use the team's scenario records, not polished sample data.

### Bring a concrete scenario

Prepare a short package.

- One task definition, including its operating scope and any qualification condition.
- Two people: the usual inspector with complete evidence, and an alternate awaiting assessment.
- One incomplete assessment record.
- A replacement scenario in which the usual person is absent.
- The decision states and the evidence rules the team agreed.

Ask the supplier to show how a supervisor would distinguish the usual inspector from the alternate awaiting assessment, what the screen says about the missing assessment, and what happens when the supervisor tries to assign the alternate. Require a traceable demonstration of the decision and its exception, from the status to the evidence behind it.

**Illustrative example:** The hypothetical manufacturer asks ICC to show how a supervisor would tell the usual inspector from an alternate awaiting assessment, then to record a practical assessment and show how the status changes. The evaluation lead notes each step shown, each step described only, and each answer promised in writing.

### Separate observation from promise

Record the outcome in the results sheet with the dispositions used earlier: shown, shown with configuration, described but not shown, and not available. A promise of a future feature is not an observed capability. If a critical decision path cannot be demonstrated, the team should proceed only with a written commitment and a way to verify it, seek clarification, or reject the option.

Keep the written answers with the evaluation record. When a question is answered in an email, copy the answer into the results sheet with the date and the name of the person who gave it, so that the commitment can be found later. If the team goes ahead, those answers become the basis for the configuration plan and the pilot scenarios, and any that are later contradicted should be raised with the supplier promptly.

### Close the loop

After the session, send the supplier the completed results sheet and the list of open questions, and agree dates for written answers. Ask the attending supervisors and assessors whether the demonstrated workflow fits their day. Then decide whether to proceed, seek clarification, or reject the option, and record the reasoning.

Teams that reach this point have a mapped workflow, defined decision states, a matrix with agreed levels and evidence rules, a plan for migration, and a way to review results.
